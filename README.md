# E-Commerce Customer Segmentation Using RFM and K-Means Clustering

## Project Title
**Advanced Business Analytics & Machine Learning Pipeline for Enterprise Customer Segmentation**

---

## Business Problem
Modern e-commerce organizations interact with extensive, heterogeneous customer bases exhibiting varied purchasing cadences, financial values, and structural brand engagement patterns. Treating all consumers uniformly yields sub-optimal marketing returns, high churn exposure among premium accounts, and inefficient capital distribution across promotional frameworks.

The objective of this analytics initiative is to transform unpolished, transaction-level purchase registers into distinct customer archetypes. By grouping users by behavior, the company can deploy data-driven targeted marketing campaigns, optimize customer retention strategies, and accurately isolate high-value accounts.

---

## Dataset Description

### Data Structural Profile
The asset comprises historical line-item ledger registers detailing sales transactions over a continuous operational timeline. Each individual data observation row characterizes a specific product transaction line item within a designated transaction event.

### Feature Matrix Definitions
* **InvoiceNo:** A unique 6-digit structural string sequence identifier assigned to each individual sales transaction event.
* **StockCode:** A distinct item product code alphanumeric sequence allocated to individual unique physical inventory components.
* **Description:** A text descriptive name string clarifying the exact commercial item type.
* **Quantity:** The total volume units of the specific commodity provisioned per individual transaction row entry.
* **InvoiceDate:** The exact temporal calendar date and timestamp record indicating when the transaction event occurred.
* **UnitPrice:** The raw numeric currency asset valuation price per single unit item of inventory.
* **CustomerID:** A distinct 5-digit consumer identification profile number sequence unique to each account.
* **Country:** The national geopolitical state identity of the customer's operational registration.

### Business Analytical Boundaries
* **Addressable Questions:** Historical performance review, identifying core operational market spaces, isolation of premium high-spending accounts, finding high-volume inventory items, and evaluating customer engagement trends.
* **Unaddressable Questions (Missing Constraints):** Customer acquisition costs (CAC), net marketing spend tracking, digital campaign attribution, user experience issues, product return reasons, or profit margins (COGS).

## Data Cleaning Summary
To construct a trustworthy mathematical model, we executed the following data cleaning checklist:

1.  **Account Identity Isolation:** All transaction records lacking a valid `CustomerID` were pruned, as anonymous events cannot be consolidated into individual customer profiles.
2.  **Structural Null Erasure:** Empty descriptive entries within the `Description` vector were eliminated to protect data integrity.
3.  **Deduplication Sequence:** Duplicate logs representing identical transactional entries were removed.
4.  **Operational Corrections Handling:** Records showing negative or zero values for `Quantity` or `UnitPrice` (which indicate order cancellations, returns, or operational system adjustments) were removed to ensure all metrics represent actual positive cashflow.
5.  **Data Type Cast Validation:** `CustomerID` was converted into a uniform string sequence, and `InvoiceDate` was parsed into standardized datetime objects for accurate tracking.

---

## Feature Engineering Summary
We transformed the raw transactional engine logs into a clean summary table with one row per customer, using the following calculated metrics:

* **Total Revenue per Customer ($M$):** The complete financial cashflow value generated by an individual customer account, calculated as:
    $$\text{Monetary Spend} = \sum (\text{Quantity} \times \text{UnitPrice})$$
* **Total Number of Purchases ($F$):** The total frequency of unique order IDs placed by the account, capturing purchase consistency over time.
* **Total Quantity Purchased:** The sum total of all items moved by the buyer across their lifetime history.
* **Average Order Value (AOV):** The mean financial spend calculated per transactional invoice instance.
* **Unique Products Cataloged:** The total count of unique stock codes purchased, mapping out customer taste diversity.
* **Geopolitical Origin Vector:** Retaining the customer's country of registration for regional filtering.

## EDA Insights

* **Geopolitical Distribution:** The overwhelming majority of enterprise revenue is generated in the United Kingdom. International distribution channels represent small tactical testing markets rather than stable core revenue drivers.
* **Inventory Velocity Performance:** A small group of high-volume products dominates the order records, while a large tail of catalog items moves infrequently.
* **Variance Profiles & Outliers:** Both Unit Price and Quantity distributions show strong right-skewness. The presence of extreme luxury transactions or bulk commercial buying creates clear outliers that require log transformation before modeling.

---

## Clustering Approach
We built an algorithmic segmentation model using **K-Means Clustering** applied to the customer-level RFM data matrix:

1.  **Variance Stabilization:** We transformed the highly skewed RFM metrics using a mathematical log function ($\log(x + 1)$) to normalize the distributions.
2.  **Feature Normalization:** We used standard scaling to convert the metrics to a mean of zero and variance of one. This ensures that large financial numbers do not overshadow lower frequency counts during distance calculations.
3.  **Hyperparameter Selection:** We evaluated the cluster configurations using the **Elbow Method**, plotting within-cluster sum of squares (Inertia) across a range of $k$ values from 1 to 10.
4.  **Optimal Configuration Determination:** The resulting line chart showed a clear inflection point or "elbow" at **$k = 3$**, establishing it as the ideal number of segments for this customer base.

---

## Cluster Interpretation

Based on our model's mathematical results, the customer base is cleanly divided into three distinct behavioral identities:

### Cluster 0: At-Risk Occasional Buyers
* **Behavior Profile:** Long stretches of inactivity combined with low transaction frequency and minimal total spend.
* **Metrics Summary:** High Recency (long time since last purchase), Low Frequency, Minimal Monetary value.
* **Business Valuation:** Low immediate revenue contributors, but they represent latent database capacity that can be reactivated.

### Cluster 1: Developing Core Customers
* **Behavior Profile:** Moderate purchasing consistency with steady, average spending habits and recent interaction history.
* **Metrics Summary:** Low-to-Moderate Recency, Moderate Frequency, Steady Mid-Tier Monetary value.
* **Business Valuation:** The operational engine of the business. This segment provides consistent baseline volume and offers the highest growth potential for upselling.

### Cluster 2: High-Value Loyal VIPs
* **Behavior Profile:** Exceptional transactional consistency, frequent interactions, recent purchases, and high financial expenditure.
* **Metrics Summary:** Exceptionally Low Recency (very active recently), Extremely High Frequency, Tremendous Monetary value.
* **Business Valuation:** The critical financial pillar of the company. A small percentage of accounts in this cluster generate a large share of total enterprise revenue.

## Final Business Recommendations

To maximize marketing returns and customer retention, we recommend the following tailored strategies based directly on our cluster profiles:

### 1. Retention Strategy for High-Value Loyal VIPs (Cluster 2)
* **Insight:** These customers drive the business's profitability but are highly attractive targets for competitors.
* **Actionable Recommendation:** *Customers in Cluster 2 have high monetary value and exceptionally frequent purchase records. The company must enroll them in an exclusive premium loyalty framework with dedicated account support, early access to new inventory, and no-threshold delivery benefits to lock in their long-term loyalty.*

### 2. Upselling Strategy for Developing Core Customers (Cluster 1)
* **Insight:** These users are satisfied with the brand but have not yet maximized their potential wallet share.
* **Actionable Recommendation:** *Customers in Cluster 1 exhibit highly stable transactional cadences but moderate average order values. The company should deploy automated cross-category product recommendations and tiered threshold validation discount structures (e.g., "Spend $100 more to receive $20 off") to systematically boost their order sizes.*

### 3. Reactivation Strategy for At-Risk Occasional Buyers (Cluster 0)
* **Insight:** Standard marketing emails will likely be ignored by this cold segment; they require direct, high-incentive outreach.
* **Actionable Recommendation:** *Customers in Cluster 0 show long periods of inactivity combined with minimal financial value. The company should deploy automated win-back email campaigns featuring steep discounts on items they previously viewed, combined with urgency-driven subject lines to encourage reactivation.*
